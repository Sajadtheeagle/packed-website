How to Rank Higher on Google Maps as a Contractor (2026)

Google only ranks your listing on three things. Here's what they are — and the exact moves that get an Ottawa contractor into the top 3.

Get My Free Audit

When an Ottawa homeowner's AC dies or a pipe lets go, they don't scroll ten results — they search “plumber near me,” look at the map, and call from the top three. Roughly 70% of those calls go to the map pack, which makes it the single most valuable piece of real estate in contractor marketing. So how do you rank higher on Google Maps? Google has published the answer, and it's shorter than most agencies want you to believe.

Google ranks your listing on exactly three things

Per Google's own documentation, local ranking comes down to relevance (does your profile clearly say you do what was searched?), distance (how close you are to the searcher), and prominence (how well-known and well-reviewed you are — reviews, links, mentions). You can't move your shop, so every tactic below is really just relevance or prominence in work boots.

1. Get your primary category exactly right

The primary category is the strongest relevance signal you control. “HVAC contractor,” “Plumber,” “Electrician” — pick the one that matches your money service, then add secondary categories for the rest. We regularly see Ottawa trades sitting on a generic category (“Contractor”) and wondering why they're invisible for “furnace repair.” This is a five-minute fix that can move you more than months of anything else.

2. Fill in every service — in the words homeowners use

Under each category, Google lets you list services (“furnace repair,” “drain cleaning,” “panel upgrade”). List all of them, named the way a homeowner would type them — not internal jargon. Add your real service areas (Ottawa, Kanata, Orleans, Barrhaven, Nepean) rather than one giant radius.

3. Reviews: steady beats big

Reviews are the heart of prominence. Three things matter: count, recency, and replies. A steady 2–3 new reviews a month outperforms a one-time blast of 20, because Google reads velocity as a sign you're actively in business. Ask at the moment of thanks, text the direct review link within 10 minutes of finishing the job, and reply to every review — including the bad ones. Published local-SEO industry studies consistently rank review signals among the top map-pack factors, ahead of almost everything people usually obsess over.

4. Photos and posts: proof you're real and active

Upload real job photos — trucks, crews, before/after — and add new ones monthly. Listings with regular photo activity earn more calls and direction requests, and fresh activity is another liveness signal. A weekly post (a finished job, a seasonal tip, a rebate note) takes five minutes.

5. Your website still matters — for the map

Google cross-references your listing with your site. A page per service and per area (“AC repair Kanata”), your name-address-phone consistent everywhere it appears online, and a fast mobile site all feed relevance and prominence. This is also where citations — consistent listings on directories like YellowPages, 411.ca and the BBB — quietly help.

What doesn't work (and can get you suspended)

Keyword-stuffing your business name (“Mike's Plumbing — Best Cheap Plumber Ottawa 24/7”), fake addresses to appear in more suburbs, and bought reviews all violate Google's guidelines. Suspensions are common, appeals are slow, and while you're suspended you get zero calls. Don't.

How long it takes

With the basics fixed and reviews flowing, most contractors see movement in 60–90 days — faster in less competitive trades and suburbs, slower for “plumber Ottawa” downtown. The channel is slow to build and very hard for competitors to take away once you hold it. How long does it take to rank in the Google map pack?

Typically 60–90 days of consistent work — correct categories, complete services, steady reviews and monthly photos — with competitive downtown trades taking longer and suburban service areas often moving faster.

Do I need a website to rank on Google Maps?
