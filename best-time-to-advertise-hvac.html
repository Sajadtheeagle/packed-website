Best Time to Advertise an HVAC Business in Ottawa (2026)

The honest answer: advertise before the season turns, not during the panic. Here's the month-by-month demand calendar for an Ottawa HVAC company — and where to put your budget.

Get My Free Audit

If you only advertise when the phone goes quiet, you're already late. HVAC demand in Ottawa is seasonal and predictable — air conditioning peaks in the summer heat, heating peaks once the cold sets in — so the best time to advertise an HVAC business is the few weeks before each season breaks, while your competitors are still asleep and ad costs are lower. Below is the calendar we work from, with real Ottawa timing and the budget logic behind it.

The short answer

Advertise heaviest in the two to three weeks ahead of each demand spike: mid-to-late May for cooling, and September into early October for heating. That's when homeowners start thinking about comfort but haven't yet had the emergency. Booking those people early means you're the company they call when the first heat wave or cold snap hits — instead of fighting every other contractor for attention (and paying peak ad prices) on the same day.

Ottawa's HVAC demand calendar, month by month

Ottawa has one of the widest temperature swings of any major Canadian city — humidex past 40°C in July, wind chill past –30°C in January — which makes the seasonal pattern sharper here than in milder markets. Roughly:

  • March–April (shoulder): Quiet. Maintenance and tune-up season. Cheap clicks, low competition — good for brand and review-building, not emergency volume.
  • May–June (cooling ramp): The single best window to advertise AC installs and repairs. Demand is climbing, costs haven't spiked yet, and a system quote placed now turns into a booked install before July.
  • July–August (cooling peak): Phones ring on their own during heat waves. Advertise to capture overflow and emergency calls, but expect higher costs-per-click as everyone bids.
  • September–October (heating ramp): The best window for furnace tune-ups, replacements and the fall rebate push. Get in before the first frost.
  • November–February (heating peak): Emergency furnace season. High intent, high competition. This is where Local Services Ads and a fast-answering phone earn their keep.

Summer is here — what to do right now

It's late June. AC demand is climbing toward its July peak, so your job today isn't to start from zero — it's to capture the homeowners actively searching. Make sure your Local Services Ads and Google Business Profile are live and showing for “AC repair” and “air conditioner installation” searches, because cooling-related queries climb sharply once Ottawa hits its first stretch of 30°C-plus days. Lean into emergency-intent keywords now, and quietly start collecting reviews from every summer job — those reviews are what will carry you into the fall heating season.

The shoulder months are your secret weapon

Most HVAC owners go dark in spring and early fall because the phone is quiet. That's exactly why those weeks are valuable: published industry benchmarks consistently show cost-per-click and cost-per-lead drop in the off-peak months because fewer competitors are bidding. Advertising a furnace tune-up special in September, or an AC pre-season check in May, books low-cost jobs and puts your name in front of homeowners weeks before the rush — so you own the relationship before the emergency. Spending a steady amount year-round, rather than only during peaks, almost always produces cheaper leads on average.

Don't confuse “busy” with “no need to advertise”

The most common mistake is shutting ads off in July because you're slammed. The problem: the moment you stop, you vanish from the searches, and the leads you would have captured for next month go to whoever stayed visible. A better approach is to adjust, not switch off — trim budget during the peak if you're at capacity, but keep your listing and a small always-on campaign running so the pipeline never goes cold. Demand drops fast after a heat wave; your visibility shouldn't.

How much to budget, and where to put it

There's no single right number, but a useful rule of thumb from published industry data is that home-service companies spend somewhere in the range of 5–10% of revenue on marketing, weighted toward the weeks before each season. For most Ottawa HVAC contractors the highest-return order is: first your Google Business Profile and reviews (free, drives the most calls), then Local Services Ads for emergency intent, then Google Search Ads for installs, then a steady content and follow-up engine underneath it all. Put the heavier ad dollars into the May and September ramp windows, and keep a smaller always-on amount the rest of the year.

The honest part

The best time to advertise an HVAC business isn't a single magic date — it's a rhythm: ramp before each season, stay visible through the peak, and use the quiet months to build reviews and capture cheap leads. When should an HVAC company start advertising for summer in Ottawa?

Mid-to-late May, before the first real heat wave. Demand and ad costs both climb through June and July, so booking homeowners early means you're the company they call when their AC quits — at a lower cost than bidding against everyone on the hottest day of the year.

Should I pause my HVAC ads when I'm already busy?

Adjust rather than pause. If you're at capacity during a heat wave, trim budget but keep your Google listing and a small always-on campaign live. If you go fully dark, you disappear from search and lose next month's leads to whoever stayed visible.
